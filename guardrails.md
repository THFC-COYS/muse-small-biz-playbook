# Guardrails: the approval rule

The entire playbook rests on one principle, enforced by Muse itself:

**Nothing publishes, sends, or spends without the owner's approval.**

Muse works in its own isolated space and checks with you before it publishes content, sends messages, spends money, or shares data. That is the boundary that makes an agent workforce safe to deploy in a business where a wrong autonomous action has immediate commercial consequences.

## How to configure it

1. Connect each service (Shopify, Stripe, QuickBooks, Slack, Asana, Notion) through Muse's connector setup. Start with **read access** everywhere.
2. Give Muse a standing goal in plain language, e.g. "Keep my books clean and flag anything that looks off."
3. When Muse proposes an external action, it will ask first. Approve or reject case by case until you trust the pattern, then approve by category.
4. Never grant a standing pre-approval on anything that moves money or talks to customers. That approval is the job you keep.

## What the owner keeps

The owner keeps every consequential decision. The agent does the finding, the drafting, the reconciling, the summarizing. You do the deciding. A 30-person company operating like 300 still has one person signing the checks. That's you.

## What to watch

- **Connector scope creep**: review connected services monthly. Disconnect what you don't use.
- **Draft quality**: spot-check Muse's drafts (ad copy, customer replies) weekly at first. The agent learns your voice from your corrections.
- **Approval fatigue**: if you're approving 50 things a day, the workflow is wrong. Push the pattern back a step until approvals cluster around real decisions.
